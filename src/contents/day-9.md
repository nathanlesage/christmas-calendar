There is much to like about Baroness. From their artful album covers that are custom-commissioned for every record, their vibe, or the creative interpretation of their genre. Baroness is quite an interesting band, although I have to admit that I never listened to them during my Spotify/Apple Music era, because their songs were recommended very rarely. Baroness is one of these bands that, at least for me, heavily benefit from the mere fact that it is much more likely to unearth them during random searches across my library.

As with many other bands, I strongly recommend you to also give a listen to the Red Album and Blue Record (you may spot a pattern), but I am recommending Yellow & Green since it is the fullest version of their sound. I have to admit that I haven’t listened to their newer albums (such as purple, and their most recent album, which breaks the colorful streak, “Stone”), so the following information may be somewhat outdated.

The album (but also their entire musical style) is very mid-heavy with few highs and lows — essentially what you’d expect when someone calls their music “sludge” metal. But this is not to say that it is bad. To the contrary, Baroness has a very mellow and comforting sound. From the mixing on the album, you’d never expect the amount of energy they bring to the stage. But, what is more important, unlike some of the albums I’ve been recording here, Yellow & Green (like their other albums) actually have strongly distinguishable songs, which makes it easier to name a favorite song (including one honorable mention), but which makes it harder (in a good way), to just pick those songs.

No, Yellow & Green still manages to tell a story, from the beginning to the end, and only listening to your favorite songs would do their album wrong. So when I tell you my favorite songs, please don’t take this as a recommendation only for those. Little Things, for example is just as well-placed after March to the Sea, as is Twinkler, Cocainium, … you get the idea. As I am listening to the record while writing this review I realize that I need to be listening to Baroness much more often than I do. And so should you!

A final word: Even though Baroness makes Sludge Metal, it is very compatible with Indie, so I think it is not just recommendable to a bigger group of readers than just those who instinctively react to the term “metal.”
