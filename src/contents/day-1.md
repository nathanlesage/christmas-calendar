I’m going to start this series with an album that may or may not surprise you if you know me personally, and which will feel a bit like an exception as we continue to go through these recommendations over the coming month.

The way I discovered this record was through a colleague, Jens, who is currently a Postdoc in London. He recommended me this album many years ago, I think probably 2014 or 2015. Overall, the album is interesting, because on the one hand, it ticks all the boxes that I personally think make an excellent record, but on the other hand, it only contains one absolute “banger” song, Humiliation, and a bunch of good but forgettable tracks.

I include the album here, and do count it towards one of my favorites, primarily because of this seeming contradiction. The reason is that all songs leading up to Humiliation build up a very peculiar mood. Every song contributes to a specific feeling that simply takes a good 20 minutes of continuous listening before it sets in. And then “Humiliation” starts — with all the prelude, the song comes in like a wrecking ball, to stretch this already strenuous metaphor a bit further.

So let’s talk a bit more about the most important part of this album: track number eleven. What sets this song apart from many others is its progression, and the juxtaposition of hectic and calm. Across the five minutes runtime, we hear the same, driving drum beat, which forms the backdrop for a half-tempo melody and accompanying vocals. The song does not have a classical verse/chorus structure, and instead keeps a constant tension, and slowly fills the silence with more and more layers of sound, none of which make it feel really “maximist” as some progressive songs typically go, but which rather round the sharp edges of the drum-vocal juxtaposition. One might even go so far as hearing some faint post-rock elements here and there, especially when the guitars, which are notably absent on the front-end of the song, provide some heavily delayed and reverberated notes in the background. The last minute then does something very interesting: while maintaining all the drive and core elements of the first four minutes of runtime, the entire mood switches merely with a change in harmonics.

Overall, I think that some of you may appreciate the album more than I do, because it so notably sits outside of the “typical” genres I appreciate. But that is precisely the reason I placed this album in the beginning: Because it offers a different baseline than some of the other possible choices I could’ve made, and therefore allow a better contrast to understand the upcoming selections.
