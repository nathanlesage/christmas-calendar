I know a lot of people who look at me and immediately go like “Huh, you listen to metal, huh?” And, well, contemplating the selection of music I have picked for this calendar, one might be tempted to agree with that. But to be fair, I believe this is a form of selection bias. Pop musicians are less likely to be on Bandcamp, and alternative genres from Indie to Metal are *much* more likely to place their music on Bandcamp. It has an undeniable cultural connotation for mostly heavier music. But not entirely. There are some amazing gems to be found there.

Snarky Puppy is a band that I actually did not discover on Bandcamp, though. I discovered them via the YouTube algorithm, because one of my favorite live recording is indeed their [song Lingus, recorded by GroundUP Music NYC](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=L_XJ_s5IsQc). I don’t fully remember when I started enjoying fusion, but I think it may have started at some point during my graduate studies. I remember there were a few fellow students (participating in the political science master, not the sociology one that I enrolled in), who at some point asked me whether I’d like to contribute to a bit of freestyle Jazz at an open air in Bonn. I said yes, and it turned out to be quite fun. The other explanation I have is that fusion isn’t technically that far away from the technical death metal style that Between the Buried and Me or Protest the Hero do.

Now, disagree all you want with me, but I feel the particular style of playing music way too fast for me to follow suit, but with razor sharp precision is something that will never get boring to my ears. Regardless of whether it’s fusion supergroups like Snarky Puppy or some maniac metal guitarists from the Midwest. So enjoy an excellent record that I listen to at least once a month or something like that. Both Lingus and Shofukan are absolute bangers. Go, it’s free to listen to!
