Kvelertak, ah, Kvelertak. What a lovely bunch of folks. I discovered them when this very album flew into our radio studio back in Bonn — one of the first (newly minted) Vinyls that I ever listened to. And it’s a joy to listen to. Kvelertak are a bunch of Norwegians that both liked Black Metal, but also good grooves. So why not combine them? That’s how you get this genre that can alternatively be described by … maybe … Party Black Metal? In any case, it’s a super fun group with lovely song names such as “Blood thirst” or “Mead.” Oh, by the way, that reminds me of a funny story they once told me over the phone: What does “Kvelertak” actually stand for? Well, they apparently managed to successfully seed the idea that it meant “owl penis” (because … why not, I guess), while the real meaning is much less distressing — stranglehold. And Kvelertak aren’t alone, there’s another fun group called Skambankt (“to be beaten up”) that make much more Punk-like music. Similarly fun, a bit different genre.

*Anyhow*, Kvelertak is nice and I connect many fond memories with them. First the aforementioned vinyl that we received at the radio. Second, the fact that I was able to conduct an hour-long interview when they were touring through Germany. That was when I actually learned how to record old landlines (yeah, that was before the widespread adoption of mobile phones, I know I’m old) into a radio station’s audio loop. And an interview with so many great sound bites, that I managed to cut together an almost 15-minute long super-feature of the guys. **TODO: It would be lovely to offer that to visitors.** Third the fact that they are so incredible live that, even though I missed the first 10 minutes of their show on a festival, it took me exactly 30 seconds to reach first row through the crowd.

Now, when you ask me for favorite songs, it’s a bit difficult — all of them are great starters on a Friday evening, but I think at the top we have Blodtørst, just because it just grooves, directly followed by Mjød. Also, if you like Mjød, you may want to give the movie Trolljegeren a try: It’s a mockumentary-style fantasy “horror” movie where a bunch of students get lost in the Norwegian mountains to find a bunch of troll hunters and … well, trolls. The title song? Mjød. I was *very* happy when I realized that.
