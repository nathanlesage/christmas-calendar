While I typically don't jump at trends and what I listen to is rarely widely common — one of the reasons why I think providing these 24 suggestions in this format might be interesting and give you potentially some new ideas for great music. But Angine de Poitrine is a bit different. This band I did not, in fact, discover via some concert, festival, or algorithmic suggestion, but via a good friend.

It turns out that these two guys have had a blast during the pandemic in creating quite exciting music, and a lot of people jumped on it. They've performed live at KEXP, a bunch of festivals, and became very famous almost overnight. At least that's how I perceived it. After all, I only joined in somewhat later.

To be very frank, I don't quite understand their music, but I believe that this is part of the fun. They play completely instrumental (apart from some vocals which I would strongly consider more of an additional instrument, given the stark distortion via effects), and make use of microtonal aspects in their music. What does this mean? Well, I didn't know until someone suggested I listen to King Gizzard & The Lizzard Wizard once, after which it became clear. But don't get me wrong, Angine de Poitrine sound completely different. King Gizzard & The Lizzard Wizard is an experiment in musical genres, Angine de Poitrine is an experiment with your auditory cortex. The music is very strange and difficult to listen to, but it feels extremely rewarding once you give it a listen for two or three times.

In terms of widening one's experience with music, listening to Angine de Poitrine, and especially Vol. II which sounds a bit more experienced than Vol. I, is a very good tip. I don't listen to the album often, but if I do, I do it with full mental capacity. And I have yet to lose any piece of interest into this strange, but due to this absolutely incredible music.
