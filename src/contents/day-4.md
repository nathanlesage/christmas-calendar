ISIS is one of the bands that nudged me to stray from the path of listening to Symphonic Metal and overproduced Melodic Death Metal back in my school days. I think I first discovered ISIS via their album “Wavering Radiant” which I found at a local record store (that didn’t survive Covid) in the place where I was born some time after school. While I think ISIS has made some much better albums (as evidenced by my recommendation), the band has stayed with me for quite a long time, before I moved on to other bands. The one great boon I found after switching back to my own music library is that, with limited choice, I started listening more to older titles that I had almost forgotten over the avalanche of new music that is steadily pushed into the algorithmically curated playlists of Spotify or Apple Music. It’s great to (re)discover old classics, such as ISIS. And, of course, having to think about the controversy the (U.S.-based) band had to go through after 2014, which culminated in them using their official Twitter account to reassure American citizens that they are not, in fact, a middle-eastern terrorist group.
