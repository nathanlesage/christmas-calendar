This is not going to be just a review of Coma Ecliptic, mind you. Between the Buried and Me is a band that holds a very, very special place in my mind. They are the band that has completely derailed my previous musical taste and has essentially been the template against which most of the music I consider subjectively “excellent” has been benchmarked against. I originally got to hear about them in ca. 2010, when I was visiting a school friend to chit-chat about guitars. He showed me my (still, to this date) favorite album, Colors (2007). I would’ve wanted to review this one, because it’s one of the most exceptional musical records ever released, but unfortunately, the band was with a different label back then which isn’t present on Bandcamp. To make matters worse, my second-favorite album of all times, Obfuscation (2009) suffers the same fate. But my third-favorite album fortunately was released after they switched labels, and as such is on Bandcamp — Coma Ecliptic. This album is also quite nice, because I can connect the release of Coma Ecliptic to a neat story as well. When they released it, they went on tour, and, on the way, stopped in the Westfalenhalle in Dortmund. The only issue? They weren’t the main act, but rather a supporting act for Leprous. But I didn’t care; I went there for BTBAM, but stayed to realize that Leprous was also a fantastic band. To this day I still don’t why the managers decided that the musical styles of BTBAM and Leprous were compatible (they aren’t), but I don’t care. Both solid bands, and seeing BTBAM live was exciting. I still have the (free) promo poster of Coma Ecliptic framed in my flat, and it will remain framed and on the wall in any coming flats I may occupy in the future.

But to say a bit more on the musical style: Between the Buried and Me is a bit hard to classify. They certainly make metal, there’s some clean and guttural singing, the guitars are doing things beyond my capabilities, but everything feels extremely well-fitting. But what really excites me is the fact that all records of BTBAM somehow manage to be a single grand opus that only gets chopped up into individual songs afterwards. Indeed, I remember reading somewhere that Colors was originally a single, 50 minute song that only got sliced up because the label didn’t want an album with only one song. They very obviously have never heard of the band Sleep, but I digress. What is amazing about every record BTBAM has released thus far is that it is hard to ever hear repeats. I don’t know a single song that actually follows the classical Verse-Chorus-Verse-Chorus-Bridge-Chorus structure. Instead, most riffs get repeated four times over about 20 seconds, never to be heard again. To make it even better, they always add some fantastic oddities into their compositions, such as extremely loud horse neighing in the song “Ants of the Sky” (Colors). If I had to sum up the listening experience of BTBAM with one word, it would probably be *verrückt* (close, but not equal to “crazy”).

Before this backdrop, Coma Ecliptic certainly is much more restrained. It’s still a single piece chopped up at somewhat convenient places (but you will have a subpar listening experience with any music player that cannot seamlessly play songs after each other), but it sounds more like what we would expect from a “normal” record. Is this a good shift? I don’t mind, because the vibe of the band has remained close to home for the past twenty years. But again, if you decide to give Coma Ecliptic a listen, and think that it has some merit, I’d recommend working your way backwards, until you arrive in 2007 with Colors.
