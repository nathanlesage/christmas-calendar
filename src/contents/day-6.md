**Note**: After this review has been written, The Ocean released [Solaris (2026)](https://theocean.bandcamp.com/album/solaris). It is an incredibly powerful record and I recommend giving it a listen as well. But Heliocentric (and Anthropocentric, for that matter), are equally amazing, and so I decided to keep this review.

The Ocean is another band that I have listened to between about 2010 and 2017, only to rediscover them in 2026 when I reinstated my personal music library. I don’t know what it is, but somehow the algorithms of both Spotify and Apple Music are really good at throwing new random music at you, but really bad at suggesting you bands you may have forgotten over time. I don’t think I ever got a song by The Ocean ever recommended on any of the streaming services I perused for almost a decade. One more reason to switch back to owning my own music, it seems. Anyway, The Ocean is a personal favorite of me, both for their great music, and for some deeply personal experiences I made with them. Heliocentric is a concept album focusing on philosophical concepts around the heliocentric world view (who could’ve guessed!), supported by an accompanying record, Anthropocentric (I’m not going to explain what the core theme here is), released in the same year. When deciding on which album I’d like to recommend, I chose Heliocentric, but only because it has in general a better vibe to me. Anthropocentric is a bit more sludgy and not as “tight” as Heliocentric, but again, still a very good record. After all, all of these recommendations are 100% subjective.

But there’s a related story to tell regarding The Ocean. You know, back in my undergrad, I was also a member of the local university’s radio station, which was called radio96acht back then, and later got renamed to bonnFM. After moving through the ranks and learning the ropes of moderating radio shows, I got the chance to host my very own show, “The Bleed Through.” I started reviewing primarily new metal records. Apart from various amazing perks that came with the job such as getting new records for free (such as Kvelertak’s first album and Ghost’s first album, both on vinyl, because they just got sent by labels to the station for review purposes), one peculiar perk was that I also could visit concerts as a member of the press. One of these concerts was a Cult of Luna concert in May 2013 where The Ocean was a supporting act. And that is where I took the picture I used as the background for this calendar. It certainly has a peculiar feeling to it, being able to take a DLSR camera into a concert, and stand in the pit directly in front of the band. Anyhow, that was just a cool little story I wanted to share. Onward!
