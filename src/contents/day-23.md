This is an old one for me. Back when I was a teen, I started to discover my own taste in musical genres. I made a bunch of very questionable choices but eventually settled on the metal side of the music industry. At the same time, I started to feel that the musical taste of many of my close friends was lacking diversity. As they settled in to very specific subgenres, I continued to explore. Before this exploration led me to the more eccentric genres I have collected in this list, like fusion or jazz, I of course ventured into the variety that metal as a genre has to offer. Because even though for most non-metal listening people, it may all sound like variations on “dying geese” (as my family would call it, “*Schreimusik*”), there is a surprising amount of variation, once you attempt to give it a shot.

One of the more obscure parts of metal that I discovered as early as 2008 were bands that took heavy influence from early Black Metal (which, mind you, shares *a lot* of history with Punk and Kraut rock) but combined it with melody. One of the first bands after the more obvious choices such as Finntroll, Eluveitie, or Equilibrium was Alcest. It’s a French black metal/shoegaze band that combines black metal aesthetic with strong melodic and harmonic parts, which makes them a great listening experience in my books. Alcest has a very spacious sound, a relaxing and almost dreamy atmosphere, all supplanted by intermittent reminders of their heritage.

While Les Voyages De L'Âme is a quite old record at this point, it is still my go-to for various situations. Like my Sorority Noise-suggestion I feel this record pairs well with spring evenings and summer sunsets. Even if you do not prefer more harsh genres of music, you might actually find this record quite nice to listen to — much more so than several of my other suggestions here.
